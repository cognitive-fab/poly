### S194 (Q8): accepted, with the keyring closed

A person signs every policy, as proposed: a `policy` record by a key of the `people` keyring over `{ scope, document, sources, engine }`, written by `poly sign policy`, never served over MCP, refused at admission and at load with `PL6409 POLICY_NOT_SIGNED`, and the two certificate faults `omit-policy-signature` and `stale-policy-signature`.

2. **The first ring is anchored outside the project.** The project's first `people` ring is pinned by a trust anchor the owner keeps outside the repository, `file:` only, as the admission key's location is today: the digest of that first ring. Admission refuses a project whose first certified ring does not match the anchor.
3. **Tests.** An agent key added to `people` without a `keyring` record is refused. A `keyring` record signed by the new key itself is refused. A ring changed after admission is refused at load. A policy signed by a key added that way is refused.
4. **Test keys.** The helper the twenty test files use generates its keys in the test and never writes the private key to disk. A test trust anchor is an API option for tests only, as M8's `std` option was: no command-line flag and no `poly.json` field carries it.

## Step 4: PD6, built except the refresh of stale claims, which needs a ruling

PS8.8, PS8.9 and K12 were written into the semantics first (876b4d9), as S195 to S198 ruled; PT2.4f says how they are built.

- **Evidence beside claims.** A verifier adapter returns `withEvidence(claims, { verifiedAt, statusAt, mandates })`. The evidence never enters `$claims`, whose type is the program's: it is kept with the step in `poly_evidence`, its digest is bound into the step's ledger event as `evidence`, and `poly journal verify` checks that binding.
- **Ages.** The gate computes `step.age` and `step.statusAge` from the evidence by the runtime's clock at the decision, and records them in the verdict's action, so session replay reproduces each decision with no clock. A time that is missing or after the decision gives no age (unknown). A policy writes `age(step.claims)` and `statusAge(step.claims)`, integers in milliseconds: the plan's rules chose milliseconds over a new duration literal in policy expressions, which would have changed both surfaces' grammar for one use. poly-ref's decision table explores each age read by `0`, each threshold, the next millisecond, and unknown, so the decision-table check covers ages (PD6-A3).
- **Mandates.** `chainProblem` refuses a chain that widens anywhere (a scope not within its parent's, a later expiry, a delegator that is not the parent's delegate) or that holds a revoked mandate, before any step, with an audit row: `mandate-widened` or `mandate-revoked`. PD6 says "journaled"; the plan's rules chose the audit log, as for an input outside its type (S140), since no step is taken. The worker denies `outside-mandate` before any policy when the innermost mandate does not cover the effect's kind, a constrained payload field, or the decision time (S199). `rt.revokeMandate(id)` revokes a mandate and every mandate beneath it. Policies read `step.mandate.delegate` and `step.mandate.delegator`.
- **Tests first.** `test/pd6.test.mjs`, six tests, failed before the code: PD6-A1 (a stale status is denied, a fresh one allowed, no times unknown and escalated by `on unknown`), A2 (the ages in the verdict, session replay reproduced), A3 (the never over ages proved in the project, and a seeded policy whose threshold never fires refuted with a witness), A4 (three widenings refused before the module, three audit rows, no step), A5 (eight refunds within the mandate allowed, the first and tenth outside it denied `outside-mandate`), A6 (a parent's revocation denies a pending refund and refuses the next dispatch). - **Checked.** The 159 measured files are identical to M8's close. The full suite: 1,830 tests and one after the M3 runs, 1,818 pass, no failure, 1 skipped and 11 waiting.

**For a ruling: the refresh of stale claims (PS8.8, S198).** PS8.8 has an escalation over an age ask for a fresh presentation, and the intent proposed again under a new `sseq`, within its own bound. An escalated intent leaves `Escalated` today only by `Approve` (a person's signed approval, whose re-proposal is allowed or denied, never escalated again) or by `EscalationTimeout`. A refresh is a third way out, so it needs a transition of the `OutboxIntent` model (PT9.7), with a counter for its bound. That model is `conformance/m4/outbox.poly`, a measured file, and the runtime pins its digests per bound (`RUNTIME_MODEL_DIGESTS`). So its state counts change, which the ruling on how M9 runs brings to the owner before the change. **Proposal:** a transition `Refresh` from `Escalated` to `Proposed`, taken only by `rt.refreshClaims(intent, credentials)` when the verifier gives fresh evidence, with a counter `refreshes: 0..R` (R the `staleClaims` bound, by default `Recheck`'s, 2) and `Denied` with reason `stale-claims` when it is spent; the model rechecked (invariants, deadlock freedom, `settles` under its fairness) and its digests pinned again. **Alternative:** no refresh in the runtime: a stale presentation is denied (as the fixture's rule does), and the host dispatches the action again with fresh credentials, a new step and a new intent, so the bound is the host's. Until it is ruled, PD6 is built without the refresh, and `test/pd6.test.mjs` says so.

## Stale claims: denied, not refreshed (owner, 2026-10-06)

The alternative is ruled: **no refresh in the runtime.** A presentation whose claims are older than the policy allows is denied with the reason `stale-claims`, journaled, and never escalated on its age. The host dispatches the action again with a fresh presentation; that is a new intent with its own id (`<intent>#<attempt>`, S173), checked by the gate like any other.

- **Why.** It is fail closed, adds no transition to `OutboxIntent`, keeps `conformance/m4/outbox.poly` and its pinned digests unchanged, and needs no new runtime call that takes credentials. A refresh inside the runtime would be a third way out of `Escalated` and a new path that handles credentials, for a gain the host already has.
- **Text first.** PS8.8 and S198 are amended to say so before the code: an escalation may still be raised on other grounds, but age alone denies. The retry bound for a refresh is removed; a host that re-dispatches is bounded by its own logic, and each attempt is journaled.
- **Tests.** A stale presentation is denied `stale-claims` and journaled; the same action dispatched again with fresh claims is allowed; a stale presentation is never escalated. The measured files must stay identical.
- **PD6 is accepted as built**, with this ruling.

**`poly bench gate`'s run key is accepted as recorded:** its key is generated for the run, held in memory, anchored as a test anchor in that process only, so its certificate cannot be loaded outside it.

## Step 5: `credential` and `uses`, built (S203)

PT10e was written first (0251017), renumbered from a clash with the provider's clock (420bcb6), and amended before the code to say what Stripe documents (4904dbd).

- **Both surfaces.** `credential c: oauth2 { provider "p"  grant authorization_code [pkce] | client_credentials  scopes { s, ... }  holder Principal }` is a declaration, and `uses c { s, ... } for p` an effect item. Each clause of a credential is written once, or the parse refuses it. The embedded forms are `credential(name, { kind, provider, grant, pkce?, scopes, holder? })` and `uses(credential, [scopes], holder?)`.
- **The provider profile.** `std/credentials/providers/stripe-connect.json`, with `PROVENANCE.md` beside it, was read from Stripe's Connect OAuth reference on 2026-10-06. poly-check finds a profile under the same std as the program's modules and passes it to the checker and the lowering as data, with the digest of its bytes; poly-ref reads the file on its own. A provider name that is not a plain file name has no profile, so `provider "../x"` reads nothing.
- **The checks.** `PL6821` (a scope the credential does not declare), `PL6822` (a person's grant used with no holder) and `PL6823` (an egress target outside the provider's base addresses). A provider with no profile, a scope the profile does not define, an unknown credential and an unknown holder parameter are `PL1001`. A holder that is not a `Principal`, a holder on the service's own credential, `pkce` or a grant the provider does not support, and an effect with `uses` and no egress target are `PL1201`. The credential an effect uses joins its signature, so effects that share a kind use one credential (S41).
- **Lowering and read-back.** Each credential an effect uses is written to `.poly/build/credentials/<c>.json`, and the effect's manifest entry gains `uses`. poly-ref reads both back; `credential-scope-added`, `credential-holder-dropped` and `credential-base-widened` are in the explicit list of toolchain faults, each caught with `PL2501`. Admission certifies each descriptor as it certifies a channel's (`poly.credentials` in the certificate, its digest under `artefacts`), and load refuses one changed after admission.
- **Decided by the plan's rules.** The descriptor is certified now, though the vault adapter that reads it comes at step 7, since the standing rule certifies every artefact the runtime will read. Effects that share a kind must use the same credential: the manifest is keyed by kind (S41).

## S210: what admission and load remember (for a ruling)

The review found that admission and load trust the project's own files for six things a person decides. Each is the security model's, so each waits for a ruling.

- **D1. Load does not verify the keyring chain.** Admission verifies the chain from the anchor outside the project to the rings it certifies; load trusts the certificate's rings because the admission key signed them. Whoever holds the admission key (in development, the agent's environment) can write a certificate whose people ring holds the agent's own key, sign a policy with it, and load. Reviewer D did so (`.poly/reviewD/load-forged.mjs`). *Proposal:* load verifies the chain from the anchor, as admission does, and refuses a certificate whose rings are not its head.
- **D2. The keyring history can be rolled back.** The anchor holds the first ring; a history cut back to an earlier head (with a person since removed) verifies. *Proposal:* the anchor's directory also keeps the highest head admitted for the project, and admission refuses a chain that does not extend it.
- **D3. A keyring record is not bound to its project.** Its subject is the digests of the old and new rings, so a change signed for project A admits the same change in project B, whose rings were the same. *Proposal:* the subject names the project (its anchor's id).
- **D7. A policy can be rolled back.** An older, signed and more permissive policy is admitted again, since its signature still verifies over its document, source and engine. *Proposal:* as D2, the highest signed version per scope is kept outside the project, or the signature names a sequence number.
- **D9. The production marker is a field of `poly.json`.** An agent that edits the project can drop `"production": true`, and the development keychain is admitted again (`PL6826` no longer applies). *Proposal:* production is a property of the admission key's location or of the anchor, not of the project.
- **B3, D6. A grant request is not signed.** PT10g says the approvals folder takes `poly grant-request`'s file as written, and the review shows what that allows: anyone who can write to the folder asks again for a grant that is `Granted`, which `Reauthorize`s it to `Requested`, so every intent that uses it is `credential-unavailable` until the holder consents again. *Proposal:* a grant request is a record of the kind `grant-request`, signed by a key of the people ring, as a revocation is.

Until the ruling, the build does as the specs say, and none of the six is changed.

## S210 ruled, and the gate (owner, 2026-10-07)

All six proposals are accepted. They close one gap: the project's own environment could undo what a person decided. The rule behind all six: **what a person decides is anchored outside the project and only moves forward.**

- **D1: load verifies the keyring chain from the anchor**, as admission does, and refuses a certificate whose rings are not the chain's head. Holding the admission key is no longer enough to widen the people ring. Reviewer D's forged load (`.poly/reviewD/load-forged.mjs`) becomes a test that must be refused.
- **D2 and D7: no rollback.** The anchor's directory (outside the project, under the owner's file permissions) keeps the highest keyring head admitted for the project, and the highest signed version of each policy scope. A policy signature names a sequence number for its scope. Admission and load refuse a chain that does not extend the kept head, and a policy whose sequence is lower than the kept one. Tests: the cut-back history and the older permissive policy are both refused.
- **D3: a keyring record names its project** (the anchor's id). The change signed for project A is refused in project B, with a test.
- **D9: production is a property of the anchor**, not of `poly.json`. The anchor's directory records whether the project is production; `poly.json` cannot set or clear it. With it set, the development keychain is refused (`PL6826`) whatever `poly.json` says. Test: removing the field from `poly.json` changes nothing.
- **B3 and D6: a grant request is a signed record** (kind `grant-request`, by a key of the people ring), as a revocation is. An unsigned file in the approvals folder is refused `not-a-record` and journaled; a `Granted` grant cannot be pushed back to `Requested` by anyone who can only write to the folder.
- **`LoadOptions.fault`** is refused in a production project, so no test hook is reachable there.

The PT12.5 and PT10g text is updated first. The anchor's directory format is written into the spec with its fields (first ring, head, per-scope policy sequence, production flag).
