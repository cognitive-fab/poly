## Step 1: PS18, routes and budgets, written before the code

Section 6 of the plan, ruled, is written into `04-semantics.md` as PS18, beside `route-exhausted` among PS3.2's reserved reasons and K15 in PS10's table. Decided by the plan's rules, each with its reason:

- **Numbering.** The section is PS18, and PS17 is left to M10, which plans memory as the next surface, so the two sections merge without touching the same lines. The PT text of this milestone is `PT10r` for the same reason.
- **The modules.** As a credential means `m.c` (PS16), a route means `m.r`, `std.routing` with `Route` instantiated for the route, and a budget `m.b`, `std.routing` with `Budget`. `Route` is instantiated by four rewrites named in PS18.1 (the tier range, the tiers left, the escalation condition, and failure), so the checker and poly-ref each write it from the text. `Budget` needs no instantiation: its cap is opened at run time (`Open(cap)`), so one checked machine serves every cap.
- **The budget's check (S352).** `spent + reserved <= cap + overrun` is checked on `Budget` alone, with the cap, reservations and costs in small check domains that place a reservation below, at and above the remainder, and a cost below, at and above its reservation. The amounts at run time are micro-units far above them, so the domains are `disclosed` (PT3.4): the verdict prints its instance and says what it covered, and no silent clean is claimed for amounts it did not explore.
- **Where a tier's call is governed.** A call is an intent of the route's instance, so its trust scope is the module `m.r` (PS8.7), as a channel's sends are under `m.c`. A policy governs it by naming that scope.
- **`on exhausted escalate`.** An approval does not raise a cap: an approved intent is read again, and is denied `budget` when its reservation still does not fit, so the property holds under every approval.
- **One decision per route.** A tier's map is fitted for one decision (PD1.6a), so a route serves one decision, and its calls take that decision's labels and timeout.
- **A failed call releases its reservation.** A call that fails reports no usage, so its reservation is released, as S352 says of a dead intent. What a provider bills for a failed call is outside K15, and is listed as a limit.
- **Records.** Each answered tier's call has its own `decision` event on the route's instance, with its cost. The decision's own event repeats the answering tier's and links its hash, so `poly cost` sums the calls' events and counts each cost once.

## Steps 2 to 4: money, `std.routing.Route` and `std.routing.Budget`, built (S350 to S352)

- **Money (step 2).** `@poly/core`'s `money.ts`: integer micro-units, `parseAmount` exact to six places, the price table's fields, `PL6843 PRICE_UNSOURCED` for a table without an https source or its date, and PS18.3's reservation and cost in bigint arithmetic. `test/money.test.mjs` has five tests; they failed first on the missing module.
- **`std.routing` (steps 3 and 4).** One module holds both machines, so a route's `m.r` and a budget's `m.b` each leave the other out (PS18.0). `Route` is CLEAN with 6 states, and `route-ends` holds under the fairness of `Answer` or `Fail`. `Budget` is CLEAN with 96 states over its check domains, and `within-cap` holds.
- **Decided by the plan's rules.** `Budget` first reported `PL3201`: a budget whose remainder is spent with nothing reserved accepts nothing. That is its end, not a defect, so it has the state `Spent`, terminal, entered by the step that leaves nothing reserved and no reservation able to fit (`spent >= cap`), with the invariant `spent-is-final`. A terminal state may read only control fields (`PL1401`), hence the enum `b` in place of a boolean. `Route` starts `Ready` and takes `Start()`, since an instance emits only on a step. A range type cannot be named in poly, so PS18.1's rewrite is stated over the types `0..1` of `Route`.
- **Tests first.** `test/std-routing.test.mjs` has seventeen tests: the module CLEAN with its instances printed, one seeded mutant per rule (fourteen, `route-ends`'s a route that calls a failed tier again, and loops), each a finding with a witness, `PROVENANCE.md` listing every rule as poly's own, and the tests' std holding the file byte for byte. ## Step 6: tiers as intents, reservations, settlement and crash recovery, built (S351 to S354, S356)

PT10r says what was built. Decided by the plan's rules, each with its reason:

- **The decision waits for its route.** The decision's intent is started as before and then holds its claim until its route ends, as an issued work order holds its claim (S173); a route always ends (`route-ends`), and a tier's call escalated to a person waits at the gate within the escalation's timeout, so no lease runs out under it. The route's end is taken by a sweep after every pass, which also finishes a route that ended before a crash.
- **E11 settled by test, fail closed (S353).** The runtime reads and writes the `Budget` instances itself, before the policy and in the allow's transaction; a refusal is final whatever the policy says. A policy of the route's scope reads `effect.route` and `effect.budget`, and a call reaches the policy only after the budget check, so poly-ref's decision table gives each call its route, tier and budget, once per tier.
- **A race between two calls of one budget.** The check before the policy and the reservation in the allow's transaction read each instance; when another call reserved in between and this one no longer fits, nothing is written and the call is denied `budget`. The gate's answer is then in the `verdict` with its sequence number, as for any denial.
- **Settled on every terminal transition.** The worker's `finish`, which every terminal transition takes, settles or releases in its transaction; a denial or an escalation's timeout holds no reservation, so it finds none.
- **A defect found by the 64-run test, and fixed.** Eight passes run at once each ran the sweep, and two took one route's end, the second refused by the hosted `OutboxIntent` (`PL2401`). Sweeps now run one at a time.
- **Tests first.** `test/routing.test.mjs` has seven tests: a low answer on the small tier escalates to the large one, each call its own allowed intent and decision event, the decision's event linking the answering tier's and `verifyProvenance` clean; an answer that meets the thresholds stops at the small tier; a failure escalates; a route below the thresholds on both tiers is exhausted and the decision fails `route-exhausted` with no decision event; the route's and the budget's instances refuse outside steps; a tier with no provider fails and the route goes on; and a policy of the route's scope denies the large tier by `effect.route.tier`. `test/budget.test.mjs` has three: AC26 with 64 runs over eight concurrent passes, providers held until answered so up to several reservations are held at once, every step of the budget's journal within the cap, every refusal `budget`, and every reservation settled or released once; an overrun (800,000 against a reservation of 1,600) recorded in the settlement's verdict and the instance, which is `Spent`, so the large tier's reservation is escalated, and after a person's approval denied `budget`; and a crash between the reservation and the completion, the reservation held across it and released once when the lost call dies. All failed first on the missing runtime.

## Step 7: records, replay and `poly cost`, built (S358, AC25's replay half)

- **Records.** Each answered call's `decision` event carries its tier, model, usage, cost (`{ units, unit: "micro-usd" }`, PD2.2's shape), reservation, price table digest, run and scope. The decision's own event repeats the answering tier's and links it (`via`); `poly cost` counts only the calls' events, so each cost is counted once. A call that failed has no event and no cost: its reservation was released (step 6).
- **Replay needed the std module (a defect found first).** Replay read only the project's certified sources, so a route's or a budget's machine, which poly-ref writes from `std.routing`, could not be built, and replay threw. Replay now reads every std module a build certifies among its type modules. A credential's `Grant` has the same gap, since no build records `std.credentials`, and no test replays a grant; it is listed for the v1 gate (AC27), not changed here. A build with a route or a budget now records `std.routing` among its certified type modules (S190's mechanism): load refuses a changed file, and replay reads the module from std after checking its digest, and gives poly-ref the program. This adds a binding to the certificate and weakens none.
- **`poly cost`.** A registry command, served over MCP as `poly_cost` since it reads only and takes no key; its schemas are published and PT14 lists it.
- **Tests first.** `test/cost.test.mjs` has four tests over three recorded runs (escalated and answered, answered on the small tier, exhausted): the totals per run, scope and day from the ledger alone (five calls, 42,540 micro-units) and the filters; a price table of November priced again by its digest (two large calls, 14,000) with the recorded costs unchanged, and an unsourced table refused `PL6843`; replay of every run, the three routes and the budgets included, with no provider registered and no divergence; and a completion body a provider returns beside its answer found nowhere in the store's file.
