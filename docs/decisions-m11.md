## Step 1: PS18, routes and budgets, written before the code

Section 6 of the plan, ruled, is written into `04-semantics.md` as PS18, beside `route-exhausted` among PS3.2's reserved reasons and K15 in PS10's table. Decided by the plan's rules, each with its reason:

- **Numbering.** The section is PS18, and PS17 is left to M10, which plans memory as the next surface, so the two sections merge without touching the same lines. The PT text of this milestone is `PT10r` for the same reason.
- **The modules.** As a credential means `m.c` (PS16), a route means `m.r`, `std.routing` with `Route` instantiated for the route, and a budget `m.b`, `std.routing` with `Budget`. `Route` is instantiated by four rewrites named in PS18.1 (the tier range, the tiers left, the escalation condition, and failure), so the checker and poly-ref each write it from the text. `Budget` needs no instantiation: its cap is opened at run time (`Open(cap)`), so one checked machine serves every cap.
- **The budget's check (S352).** `spent + reserved <= cap + overrun` is checked on `Budget` alone, with the cap, reservations and costs in small check domains that place a reservation below, at and above the remainder, and a cost below, at and above its reservation. The amounts at run time are micro-units far above them, so the domains are `disclosed` (PT3.4): the verdict prints its instance and says what it covered, and no silent clean is claimed for amounts it did not explore.
- **Where a tier's call is governed.** A call is an intent of the route's instance, so its trust scope is the module `m.r` (PS8.7), as a channel's sends are under `m.c`. A policy governs it by naming that scope.
- **`on exhausted escalate`.** An approval does not raise a cap: an approved intent is read again, and is denied `budget` when its reservation still does not fit, so the property holds under every approval.
- **One decision per route.** A tier's map is fitted for one decision (PD1.6a), so a route serves one decision, and its calls take that decision's labels and timeout.
- **A failed call releases its reservation.** A call that fails reports no usage, so its reservation is released, as S352 says of a dead intent. What a provider bills for a failed call is outside K15, and is listed as a limit.
- **Records.** Each answered tier's call has its own `decision` event on the route's instance, with its cost. The decision's own event repeats the answering tier's and links its hash, so `poly cost` sums the calls' events and counts each cost once.

## Step 6: tiers as intents, reservations, settlement and crash recovery, built (S351 to S354, S356)

PT10r says what was built. Decided by the plan's rules, each with its reason:

- **The decision waits for its route.** The decision's intent is started as before and then holds its claim until its route ends, as an issued work order holds its claim (S173); a route always ends (`route-ends`), and a tier's call escalated to a person waits at the gate within the escalation's timeout, so no lease runs out under it. The route's end is taken by a sweep after every pass, which also finishes a route that ended before a crash.
- **E11 settled by test, fail closed (S353).** The runtime reads and writes the `Budget` instances itself, before the policy and in the allow's transaction; a refusal is final whatever the policy says. A policy of the route's scope reads `effect.route` and `effect.budget`, and a call reaches the policy only after the budget check, so poly-ref's decision table gives each call its route, tier and budget, once per tier.

- **A defect found by the 64-run test, and fixed.** Eight passes run at once each ran the sweep, and two took one route's end, the second refused by the hosted `OutboxIntent` (`PL2401`). Sweeps now run one at a time.

- **Fixed, each with a test that failed on the code it found** (`test/review-m11.test.mjs`, nine tests, and two certificate faults in `test/certificate.test.mjs`; all eleven checked failing on the code before the fixes). PS18.4 and PT10r were amended for them.
  - **B1 (high).** A crash between the routed decision's `HandlerOk` and `SettleOk` left the decision `Settled` with its claim held for ever, and the sweep marked the route done. The sweep takes it again, and marks a route done only once its decision is terminal.
  - **B2, C1 (high).** A call whose provider answered was released with no cost and no record when its intent ended otherwise than by its completion (a crash, a refused settle, a completion outside its type). Its record is kept as the provider answers, and every terminal transition settles with its cost and writes its `decision` event if none was written. PS18.4 now says so: a call that answered is settled, a call with no answer released. The S352 crash test now asserts the lost call settled once, at its cost. A usage class named like an inherited property was a crash of the call; it is priced at the table's highest price, as any unpriced class.
  - **B3 (medium).** An escalation's timeout of a rechecked call that held its reservation left it held. It is released.
  - **B5 (low).** A route whose budget the runtime did not load ran with no reservation; it is denied `budget`, fail closed.
  - **A1 (high).** An embedded route or budget name such as `../../../x` wrote files outside the build. Names are identifiers (`PL1201`), checked before anything is lowered.
  - **A2 (high).** `exhausted-after-the-last` was violated by every route of two tiers or more whose escalation does not name `failed`, since PS18.1 makes such a failure exhaust the route at any tier.   - **A3, C6 (medium).** Two modules of a project with one route or budget name collided on the descriptor and the call's kind, and admission refused with a misleading message. Admission now refuses, naming both modules.
  - **A5 (low).** A reservation beyond the safe integers was rounded silently; it is `PL1201`.

**For a ruling: S360, a reservation and input tokens (A4).** PS18.3 reserves the tier's token limit at its highest price, and S357 passes that limit to the provider as its output limit. A call's cost counts every class, input included, so a provider that stays within its output limit is recorded as an overrun whenever its input costs more than the reservation's slack: the flash tier reserves 1,600 micro-units, and 1,000 input with 4,000 output tokens cost 1,700. The cap still holds (`within-cap` counts overruns), but K15's sentence "a cost above its reservation is a provider reporting more than it was allowed" is false for such calls. **Proposal:** a tier declares `max input tokens n` beside `max tokens`, and the reservation is `ceil((n_in × P_in + N × P_out) / 10^6)`, with the input bound passed to the adapter, which refuses a question above it before the call. **Alternative:** `max tokens` is the total, input and output, passed to the provider as such where it accepts one.

**For a ruling: S361, a usage report poly cannot price (B2, B4).** A provider that reports no usage costs nothing, so the budget never binds (fail open against K15); a report with a fractional or negative count fails the call after the provider answered, and its reservation is released. **Proposal:** both are K15 violations: the call is settled at its full reservation, and its `decision` event records `usage: unreported` or the report as given, so the budget stays closed and the violation is visible. **Alternative:** refuse such a call's answer, as an outside-domain answer is refused, and settle at the reservation.

**For the owner, not blocking: `poly try`.** Q9 was ruled on 2026-10-06 to put `poly try` (PT28.2) at the start of M11. The M11 plan, ruled on 2026-10-07, does not list it, and its scope is PT27.3's row. The session follows the later ruled plan and does not build `poly try`; it is raised again at M11's close.

## Conclusion

M11 is ruled and built. Budgets are reservations that cannot be overspent except by a provider's own overrun, which is recorded and charged against what remains: sixty-four concurrent runs never pass a cap, and a crash never loses or doubles a reservation. A route is a checked machine whose every tier choice replays from the ledger with no model call, and `poly cost` sums every answered call from the ledger alone. The review's findings are fixed, the gate passed from a clean build, and two questions about what a reservation and a cost mean wait for the owner.
