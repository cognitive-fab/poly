## Step 2: PS17 written into the semantics before the code

PS1.4b (audiences and `Items`), PS15.0a (a channel's recipients), PS17 (memory), PS11.3a (forgotten steps) and `uncited` among PS3.2's reserved reasons. Section 6 of the plan lists these changes, ruled with M11's plan. The text settles six points the plan left open, each by the plan's rules and recorded here for the owner's reading:

- **S314, the recipient's principal.** S301 names "the recipient's class and principal at the send" and S305 gives the class, not the principal. An effect names its recipient with `to p`, a `Principal` parameter, as `uses c … for p` names a credential's holder (PT10e). A channel's recipient has no principal a module names, so an owned item reaches a channel only by class (PS15.0a); a channel thread's principal is a question for the concierge example of the v1 gate, not for M10. An effect may also declare `audience c`, its recipients' default class, beside a channel's (S305).
- **S315, pending taints.** The taint of a completion's data must come from the intent it completes, or a correct concierge would be reported leaking (a completion offered with any taint) or a stale completion would carry an earlier customer's taint. Each instance keeps, as ghost state, the multiset of the taints of its retrievals' and decisions' intents not yet completed, and a completion takes one. A machine that would hold more than 3 of one kind is a bound hit, never `CLEAN`.
- **S316, `Items` is passed, not stored.** A module reads `x.count` and passes an `Items` to a decision or an effect; it is no field, record field, payload or map value. This keeps the state's shape and every existing count, and nothing in AC22 to AC24 needs one stored.
- **S317, one more principal representative** when a program declares an owned source, with the lemma in PS17.4: the owner a violation names keeps a representative of its own, distinct from the recipient's.
- **S318, a decision's answer may be a `Content`**, as PT23.1's `r.text` needs; until now PL0901 refused a decision over a `Content`, a limit of M8a that M10 lifts.
- **S319, taint per value in the check, per body at run time.** The checker carries a taint with each occurrence of a value; the runtime keeps a body's taint in the content store by digest, joined over every time it was stored. The runtime's taint is never less than the check's, so it refuses every send the flow rule refuses and may refuse more (fail closed): two customers whose messages happen to have the same bytes share a body. The flow rule is explored apart from the machine's own graph, so the ghost changes no verdict and no state count of any file.

## Steps 6 and 8: freshness, the flow check at run time, and the proposal fields `taint` and `items`

The PT text is PT10k, written first (69cf1df).

- **The flow check (S302).** The worker runs it first, before the grant check, the mandate and the policy. Each payload value that is a body's digest is a `Content`; its taint is the content store's record, and a body with none is refused `audience` (fail closed). The recipient's principal is the manifest's `recipient.to`; its class the emitting step's claims field of type `Audience` (the flow descriptor's new `claims` map, read back by poly-ref), else the declared `audience`, else the channel's, else the lowest. A refusal is final and recorded with the reason.
- **Freshness (S307).** An item set older than its source's `freshness` is denied `stale-items` (`stale deny`) or escalated `stale-items` (`stale escalate`, the default); an approved escalation is not escalated again for it.
- **The proposal and policies.** An intent carrying a `Content` is proposed with `taint: { class, owners }`, owners by keyed digest; one carrying an `Items` with `items: { count, class, ageMs }`. A policy reads `effect.taint.class` and `effect.items.count`, `.class` and `.age`; poly-ref's decision table gives each value, and none, only where a policy reads it, and the policy translation validation agrees with Polyaxion. A retrieval's and a remember's kind and labels in the policy facts are now the runtime's, as in the manifest.
- **Decided by the plan's rules.** A decision's own payload is judged for freshness and carries `items`, but is not a send: its taint goes to its answer (PS17.3). The owners in the proposal are keyed digests of `principal:<p>`, apart from the addresses' `<kind>:<address>`.

## Steps 9 and 10: remembering, verifying and forgetting, and forgotten steps on replay (S306, S309, AC24)

PT10l was written and committed before the code (cacba77).

- **Remembering (S309).** A remember is the runtime's own effect, as a retrieval is: its builtin writes each `Content` of the payload as an item of the memory source in `poly_memory` (digest, subject, provenance, class, run and intent), never the body, which stays in the erasable content store and gains the item's taint. The memory check refuses, `audience`, a remember that would write an item with a wider audience than its content, over every class and principal, as poly-ref's `PL6832` does; a body with no taint record is refused, fail closed. A retrieval from a memory source reads its items, newest first, by owner.
- **Provenance.** A proposal's `items` gains `provenance`, the lowest among its items; `effect.items.provenance` is a policy field, typed in the checker, given its three values in poly-ref's decision table where a policy reads it, and agreed by Polyaxion at admission.
- **Verify and forget.** `rt.verifyMemory` and `rt.forget` take a person's signed record (`verify` over `{ item }`, `forget` over `{ subject }` or `{ source }`) once, as a grant request is taken: not-a-record, wrong-key (an agent's key, another subject) and replayed are refused. `poly memory-verify` and `poly forget` sign them from a key file outside the repository; both are CLI only, never served over MCP, and their schemas are published. A forget erases every erasable body whose taint names the subject, or that a decision read from the source (`poly_body_source`, new), removes the item sets and memory items, asks each connected adapter to forget the subject, and records the subject by its keyed digest, or the source by name, with the erased digests.
- **Replay (PS11.3a).** Replay reads no body, so a run whose answer was erased folds as before; `replayRun` lists each step whose data names an erased body with the hash of the forget event, and `poly journal replay` prints it. It is no divergence.
- **Found and fixed by AC24's test.** `poly journal replay` reported `PL9003` on every run of a decision with `cites` (step 5): the decision event did not record the cited set, and the provenance check compared the step's data with the record by `JSON.stringify`, which reads key order. The decision record now carries `cites`, and the check compares canonically.
- **Decided by the plan's rules.** A forget of a whole source calls no adapter's `forget`, which takes a subject (PS17.7 names the adapter call for a subject). The citations of an answer that cited nothing are an empty set naming no one, and stay after a forget.
- **Tests.** `test/memory-forget.test.mjs`, five, written with the code: an answer remembered, derived, about its customer, read back by owner and verified once by a person (an agent's key, another subject and a replay refused); AC24, a customer forgotten: every body about them unreadable, their memory items and item sets gone, the source's rows deleted, the event naming them by digest only, and replay folding with the answer's step forgotten by that event and no divergence; the run-time downgrade refusal; both commands, a source forgotten, and `poly journal replay` printing the forgotten step; and a policy over `effect.items.provenance`, proved, admitted, denying an answer over observed orders, and refuted, `PL6503`, without its rule.

- **Fixed, each with a test written first that failed on the code it found** (`test/review-m10.test.mjs`). The spec text came first (6de0c758).
  - **C1.** A forget took its record before it erased, so an adapter that failed left the subject half forgotten, with no forget event, and the record used up. The record is now taken in the transaction that writes the forget event; the same record completes a forget cut short, and only then is a replay. `verifyMemory` takes its record with its event likewise.

  - **D1.** The runtime's flow check skipped every module with no flow descriptor of its own, so a second module of a program with memory sent any body, one with no taint record included. PS17.4 asks the check of every intent; PT10k had narrowed it. A module with none takes the program's one audience (PS1.4b). B's probe of a `spawn` into such a module is refused `audience` now too.

## S320 to S327: the review's findings that change the semantics or the security model (for a ruling)

- **S320 (C2). `remember s as verified`.** PS17.5 lets a program write `verified` items, so an agent that writes the module passes a policy that admits only verified items, and no person signed anything. *Proposal:* `as verified` is refused, `PL6837 VERIFIED_BY_PERSON` (from M10's codes); only `poly memory-verify` makes an item verified.
- **S321 (C3). Verify and forget records name no project.** A record's subject is the digest of `{ item }`, `{ subject }` or `{ source }`, so a forget signed for staging is taken by production when one key is in both people rings. *Proposal:* as D3 of S210, the subject includes the anchor's id.
- **S322 (A F1, F2, S4; B's spawn probe). Taint through a timer, a send or a spawn outside every configuration.** PS17.1 gives the environment's inputs `⊥` on a machine alone, and PS17.3 follows values between instances only in a configuration. So a tainted value armed in a timer, or sent or spawned to another machine with no configuration, leaves CLEAN, though the runtime refuses it. That is a silent clean of the flow rule. *Proposal:* on a machine alone, a timer's firing carries the taint its arming gave it; and the flow rule of a program with sources that sends or spawns between machines outside every configuration is listed as not run for those messages, the verdict at best `INCONCLUSIVE`, with the messages named.
- **S323 (A S1, B). A decision with a finite answer.** PS17.3 judges `Content` only, so a decision over every customer's orders whose answer is an enum or a range sends a few bits of them to anyone, CLEAN. *Proposal:* a decision's answer of any type carries the decision's taint, and the flow rule judges every payload value that has one; or, kept as it is, PS17.2 states the limit.
- **S324 (A S2). The claims class applied to another recipient.** PS17.3 takes the recipient's class from the step's claims even when `to p` names someone other than the claimant, so a staff member's step can send any customer's data to any principal. *Proposal:* the claims class is the recipient's only when the effect has no `to`, or `to` names the claimant's own principal; otherwise the declared `audience`, else the lowest.
- **S325 (A S3). `x.count` carries no taint.** PS1.4b exempts it, so whether another customer has any records reaches anyone. *Proposal:* keep it, and state the limit in PS1.4b; or give a count the taint of its set.
- **S326 (B2). Owners of a retrieval with no owner predicate at run time.** PS17.2 gives such a set the owners `Ω`, every principal; the runtime gives it the owners of the items returned. So the AC22 leak variant's reply is allowed at run time when the newest orders happen to be the asker's, and PS17.4's "at least the taint check gives" does not hold. *Proposal:* the runtime records `Ω`, which only a class above the source's reads; a forget of a subject erases a body whose owners are `Ω` only when it was read from the source being forgotten.
- **S327 (C S1). What a forget of a subject covers.** The bodies a principal sent (a question through ingress) carry `⊥` and are not erased by forgetting that principal. *Proposal:* ingress records the sender as an owner of what it stores, at the lowest class, so a forget of the sender erases it; or PS17.7 states that a forget covers what sources hold about the subject, not what the subject sent.

## S320 to S327 ruled (owner's reviewer, 2026-10-08)

- **S320: accepted as proposed.** `remember ... as verified` is refused at check as `PL6837`. Only a person's signed record, through `poly memory-verify`, makes an item verified.
- **S321: accepted as proposed.** Verify and forget records are bound to the project's anchor id, as S210's D3 binds keyring records. A record bound to another anchor is refused `wrong-project` before it is taken.
- **S322: accepted as proposed.** A timer's firing keeps the taint of the step that set it. A message between machines outside any configuration makes the flow rule `INCONCLUSIVE`, and the verdict names each such message. No silent clean.
- **S323: state the limit, and disclose it.** Typed answers stay untainted: a decision with a finite answer is a declassification point. PS17.2 says so. The certificate lists every decision that reads a tainted `Content`, with the class it reads and the size of its answer type (at most log2 of the number of answers per call). A policy can deny such a decision by `effect.taint.class`, as for any effect. Tainting finite values would mean tracking implicit flows through state, which v1 does not do.
- **S324: accepted as proposed.** The claimed class applies only when the send has no `to`, or when `to` is the claimant.
- **S325: as S323.** A count stays untainted and is a declassification point. PS1.4b states the limit, and the certificate lists each count taken over an item set whose owner predicate is not the requester's principal.
- **S326: Ω at run time, with the real owners kept for erasure.** A retrieval with no owner predicate records owners Ω for the flow rule, as PS17.2 says. Its taint record also keeps the owners of the items actually returned, used only by a forget. A forget of a subject erases every body whose kept owners include the subject, Ω or not, and every body read from a forgotten source. Erasure follows the facts; audience follows the worst case.
- **S327: record the sender at ingress.** A body received on a channel is owned by its sender's principal at ingress, class private unless the channel declares otherwise. A forget of that subject erases it. PS17.7 says so.
